# Dinosaur fact sheets

Answer field: `model` = `dino:<name>`. Example: "Erzaehl mir vom T-Rex" gives `dino:Tyrannosaurus`. Kind `dino`, source name "Wikidata". Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-12-dinosaur-factsheet-design.md`.

Status: built and tested on the computer, merged into `main` on 2026-11-14. The data is on zitro.spot. **Not tested on the stick** (see the end).

## Requirements

**What it shows.** Ask about one dinosaur, pterosaur, marine reptile of the dinosaur age or early bird by name and Zitro shows a fact sheet to page through: picture, facts, size comparison, fun fact. Pages without content are left out, and the counter counts only the pages shown. The AI only names the animal. Every value on the sheet comes from the prepared data and keeps its source (a Wikidata property, or a Wikipedia language, title, revision and sentence).

**Groups.** The note under the name comes from the animal's Wikidata lineage, never from the AI. Modern birds are excluded.

| Group | Sheets | Note DE / EN |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Non-avian dinosaurs | 1281 | none |
| Early birds (Avialae, Archaeopteryx) | 96 | `Urvogel` / `Early bird` |
| Pterosaurs | 203 | `Flugsaurier - kein Dinosaurier` / `Pterosaur - not a dinosaur` |
| Marine reptiles (plesiosaurs, ichthyosaurs, mosasaurs) | 250 | `Meeresreptil - kein Dinosaurier` / `Marine reptile - not a dinosaur` |

Total 1830 sheets (549 with a note). Early birds are dinosaurs, so they don't say "kein Dinosaurier". The notes use the ASCII hyphen. The note stands under the name on the picture page and the facts pages.

**Two tiers.**

- **Curated, 53 genera** (`scripts/make_dino_curated.py`). Picked by Wikipedia page views (Nov 2025 to Oct 2026), plus Plateosaurus (found in Frick), Maiasaura and Ichthyosaurus, with Pteranodon, Quetzalcoatlus, Plesiosaurus, Mosasaurus and Archaeopteryx required. Wikidata's numbers, plus facts copied from pinned Wikipedia revisions (219 facts and 53 fun facts, 272 in all). These can be: height (6 genera; a hip or shoulder height counts as a height), era (6 genera, only where no time range gives one: "Early Cretaceous" gives "Kreide" / "Cretaceous"), speed (9 genera), egg (1: Protoceratops), meaning of the name (52), diet, where found, year named, and a fun fact (all 53). Every fun fact names its animal, because the page above it says only "Wusstest du?". Every genus has a hand-chosen picture. The generator refuses a sentence that isn't in its stored revision, a quote that isn't in its sentence, and a fun-fact number that isn't in its source sentences. Hedges ("probably", "wohl", "vielleicht") stay in both languages.
- **Automatic, all other genera (1777 sheets).** Wikidata only: length, height, wingspan, weight, time range, year named. No speed, egg, meaning, diet, place or fun fact.

**Data rules.**

- Values that disagree (between Wikidata statements, or between de and en Wikipedia) show as a range from min to max ("12-13 m"). Never an average.
- Speed always reads "geschätzt" / "estimated". Walking speeds are left out. An egg is shown only where the source ties it to the genus.
- Wikidata gives a few sizes in feet or pounds. They are converted and rounded to as many significant digits as Wikidata's number has, at least two, so they don't look more exact than their source: 18 ft reads 5,5 m (not 5,49 m), 70 ft 21 m, 16 ft 4,9 m, 38000 lb 17 t (not 17,2 t). Metric values are kept as they are. Today this concerns Spinosaurus (height and weight), Giganotosaurus, Quetzalcoatlus and Argentinosaurus (height). Other units, short tons among them, are left out and logged.
- The ceilings (60 m length, 30 m height, 20 m wingspan, 200 t) only guard against unit errors in Wikidata. They don't judge facts.
- Every text exists in German and English. Where a source has only one language, the generator writes the other one from it, so the device never shows an empty value. German uses "ss", never "ß".
- Device text only: U+0020 to U+05FF. Ranges use the ASCII hyphen. The picture credit in the footer is ASCII.

**Files** on the Zitro server, generated by `scripts/make_dino_data.py` into `webpage2/data/dino/` (3854 files, about 9.3 MB; the largest file the stick fetches is a 13.5 KB picture):

- `index-<pp>.txt` (263 files): `ZDX1`, the first two characters of a normalised name, the count. Then `name<TAB>slug` lines, 4398 names in all: the genus, its de and en labels and its Wikidata aliases ("T-Rex"), lower case, umlauts spelled out. Species names ("Tyrannosaurus rex") lead to their genus. A name that would belong to two genera is dropped ("raptor" is not resolved). A genus name wins over another genus' alias.
- `<slug>.txt`, at most 2048 bytes: `ZDS1`, slug, QID, then lines `name`, `group` (0 dinosaur, 1 early bird, 2 pterosaur, 3 marine), `image` (length, width, height, FNV-1a hash, credit or `-`), `length`, `height`, `wingspan`, `mass`, `speed`, `egg`, `lived`, `named`, `era`, `meaning`, `diet`, `found`, `fun` (de, en, source), `source`, and a last line `end`. A sheet without `end` is refused.
- `<slug>.jpg` (1760 files): baseline JPEG, at most 240 x 117 and 32 KB.
- `credits.html`: maker, licence and file link of every picture, and a table of every curated Wikipedia sentence with its revision.

**Minimum content.** A sheet is shown only if it has its own picture or at least 2 fact rows. Otherwise `fetch()` returns false and today's Wikipedia picture shows. 70 sheets have no picture. 1226 sheets have a picture and fewer than 2 fact rows (mostly picture, name and note only), which is allowed.

**Lookup on the stick.** The AI's text never becomes a file name: the index decides. Three HTTPS requests (index, sheet, picture) have to finish inside one `EDU_LOOKUP_DEADLINE_MS` (10 s). A cut or corrupt index or sheet gives no sheet. A picture that is cut short or has the wrong hash only removes the picture page; if the sheet is then not worth showing, the normal picture shows. A name not in the index also falls back to the picture.

**Pages.** Screen 240 x 135, content 117 px high above the 18 px footer.

- *Picture.* Up to 240 x 117, name in citrus on a darkened band, the note under it, the credit in the footer. A picture is cropped to fill the screen only if that keeps 80 % of its width or height; otherwise it is shown whole (portrait skulls, fossil slabs). In the final data 575 of the 1760 pictures are exactly 240 x 117 (counted from the image lines of the sheets). A credit too wide for the footer (over 200 px) shows the licence alone, the text after the last ", ". A picture that needs no credit says "Wikimedia Commons" (186). `credits.html` always has the full credit.
- *Facts.* Name in citrus, note, then label and value rows: Länge, Höhe, Spannweite, Gewicht, Tempo (geschätzt), Ei, Zeit, Lebte, Benannt, **Bedeutet**, Nahrung, Fundort (English: Length, Height, Wingspan, Weight, Speed, Egg, Era, Lived, Named, **Means**, Diet, Found in). The label column is the widest label plus 6 px. A value wraps to at most 2 lines at 232 px; a row that doesn't fit is left out (none of the generated rows is). A page holds 5 lines and a row never splits across pages, so there are as many facts pages as needed, and at most 7 pages in all.
- *Size.* "So gross war er" / "How big it was", or "So lang war er" / "How long it was" when only a line is drawn. The animal's bar and a **170 cm person** (label "Mensch" / "Person") at one scale. The bar is the length, or the wingspan for a pterosaur with no length. A range shows as a darker end piece. With no height it is a thin line, never a guessed height. With neither length nor wingspan the page is skipped. The person is always at least 2 px tall, and everything stays inside the area. A pterosaur with only a wingspan reads "So lang war er" over "... Spannweite", because only the two headings exist.
- *Fun fact.* "Wusstest du?" / "Did you know?", the sentence, and the source in the footer ("Wikipedia (EN): Maiasaura"). All 106 fun-fact texts (53 per language) fit 5 lines at 224 px.

**Buttons.** Like the timeline. C: next page; after the last page the written answer, and the next visit starts at page 1. B: previous page, and it stops at the first. A: new question. Tilt: `timelineTiltUpdate` with `TIMELINE_TILT_SIGN`, which moved into `timeline_core.h` so both views share it. Button tabs: B shows "earlier" from page 2, C shows "later", or the text symbol on the last page.

**Written answer** (user decision 2026-11-12, "Beides"). The AI answers the question, but names no measurements, weights, speeds or dates: the sheet shows the sourced ones. It doesn't mention the sheet itself, because it answers before the lookup. The text is not replaced by the sheet. When a `dino:` lookup ran and found nothing, Zitro appends "Einen Steckbrief dazu habe ich nicht." / "I don't have a fact sheet for that." (also in the spoken answer and the memory). This reading of "Beides" was confirmed by the user on 2026-11-12.

**Fun-fact font (user decision 2026-11-12).** The fun fact is drawn in the same font as the written answers, Roboto 10 pt, 224 px wide, up to 5 lines (at 14 pt only 6 of the 106 fun facts would fit, and the bold fonts are ASCII only). The notes under the name are in the small ASCII font, because 10 pt leaves no room for them.

**No person reviewed these.** Said plainly:

- The **fun facts** and the **speeds** were chosen, translated and shortened by the AI that built the data, from Wikipedia sentences. No person read them. The generator only checks that the numbers and quotes stand in the stored sentences.
- The **pictures of the automatic tier** (1707) were looked at only by the building AI, on captioned contact sheets at their real size, every one of them. No person looked. The 53 hand-chosen pictures of the curated tier were chosen and looked at by the AI as well.
- A "no new claim" check for the fun facts was read by the AI, not by a program. A fun fact's numbers are checked, its units are not ("7 km" against "7 Meter" would pass).

**How the pictures were checked** (automatic tier, 2678 candidate genera, 1851 with a Wikidata picture):

- A free licence and a named author. Refused: 13 by licence (copyrighted free use 5, GFDL 5, bare "Attribution" 2, "No restrictions" 1), 11 for a missing author, 2 as files (one gone from Commons, one unreadable).
- A word list on the Commons categories (carcass, prey, kill, blood, wound, fight, dead ...): 3 refused, one of them a false positive ("Ardeadactylus").
- Then looked at: 1822 pictures on 38 contact sheets (118 tiles enlarged 3 times, the rest seen at real size, so a small detail could be missed). 54 refused after looking: not legible 22, predation 18, blood or wounds 5, not the animal 4, fighting 2, carcass 2, unsuitable 1.
- Pinned by SHA-1: a changed Commons file is refused until it has been looked at again.
- 48 genera lost their sheet because their picture was refused and they had fewer than 2 rows.
- The curated tier: pictures chosen by hand from Wikidata's files, the infoboxes and a Commons search, looked at on their own contact sheets, 27 of them replacing or adding to the automatic choice. Rejected for predation, blood, an unclear animal or illegibility (list in the B4 report).

**Known oddities.**

- Argentinosaurus shows Wikidata's height of 21 m (70 ft). It is sourced, but generous: English Wikipedia gives a shoulder height of 7.3 m, which is not added, so the sheet doesn't read 7,3-21 m. Its speed (7.2 km/h) is the top speed of a computer model of a pace gait.
- Stegosaurus speed reads "6-17,9 km/h, geschätzt": it merges the German article's 6-7 with the English 15.3-17.9.
- Tylosaurus rex is a species described in 2026. It is an alias of the genus, and the sheet's sizes merge both articles (8-8.4 t).
- Maiasaura lived 86.3-70.6 million years ago in English Wikipedia: that is the span of its rock formation (German says about 76.4 million).
- Some sizes are of the largest species or specimen, merged as ranges.
- Synonyms such as "trachodon" and "anatosaurus" have their own thin sheets (a picture and a note).
- "raptor" is not resolved: it names several genera.
- Many automatic sheets show only a picture, a name and a note.
- Moabosaurus has a Wikidata picture that was never looked at, so it has none.
- The curated tier leaves out walking speeds, lower bounds and group statements, and has no "found" row for Mosasaurus and Iguanodon, no meaning for Quetzalcoatlus (a god's name).
- 35 of the 53 curated sheets have no height: their pinned revisions state none for the genus, or only a feeding reach (Parasaurolophus), a reared-up pose (Iguanodon), a lower bound (Herrerasaurus, Coelophysis), a hedged one (Hatzegopteryx, "dürfte"), an old estimate (Megalosaurus 1822) or one specimen (Dilophosaurus). Their size page shows a line.
- Tyrannosaurus' height of 4 m is a hip height, from the German sentence about the specimen "Sue"; the article's general sentence says "vier Metern" in words, which the generator doesn't read.

## Release note

- Meet any dinosaur: ask "Erzähl mir vom T-Rex" and flip through a picture, the facts, a size comparison with a person and a fun fact. It works for flying saurians and sea reptiles too, and Zitro tells you when they weren't dinosaurs.
- Honest numbers: every fact on a fact sheet comes from Wikidata or Wikipedia. Where sources disagree you see a range, and speeds say "estimated". If Zitro has no fact sheet for an animal, it says so.

## Sources and licences

- **Wikidata:** names, aliases, sizes, time ranges, year named, lineage and picture links. CC0, no credit required.
- **Wikipedia (de, en):** the curated facts and fun facts, each a sentence of a named revision. Text under CC BY-SA 4.0. The fun-fact footer names the article. `credits.html` lists every sentence with its revision link.
- **Wikimedia Commons:** 1760 pictures, checked per file from Commons metadata. Licences: CC BY-SA 4.0 549, CC BY 4.0 319, CC BY-SA 3.0 299, CC BY 3.0 176, public domain 131, CC BY 2.5 116, CC BY 2.0 65, CC0 55, CC BY-SA 2.0 35, CC BY-SA 2.5 12, and three national variants (CC BY 2.5 ar, CC BY-SA 3.0 pl, CC BY-SA 2.5 it). NC, ND, fair use, GFDL alone and unrecognised licences are refused, and so is a picture with no named author, even public domain. CC BY and CC BY-SA need the maker's name: on the bottom line where it fits (else the licence alone), and always in `credits.html` with a link to the file and the note that it was resized and may be cropped. CC BY-SA pictures stay CC BY-SA after resizing. 186 pictures need no credit on the device.
- Not tidy: `credits.html` shows the author text as Commons gives it, but without e-mail addresses (Commons has them for some makers), which a public page must not republish.
- `credits.html` must be uploaded with the data, at `https://zitro.spot/data/dino/credits.html`. The website links it in its Sources list (item 15 of `webpage2/index.html`). For the 166 pictures whose credit is too wide for the stick's bottom line, it is the only place that names the maker. Whether a credit file counts as "available" enough is a decision for a person.

## Not tested, not done

- **Nothing on the stick was tested:** the drawing (including `fillRectAlpha` on the picture band, the look of the person and the DejaVu12 fallback for one name wider than 232 px, "Dromaeosauriformipes rarus"), the fetch time of three HTTPS requests within 10 s, the tilt direction, and whether the button tabs cover the counter. Host tests cover the logic, every generated file and the decoder on all 1760 pictures (`tests/dino/run.sh`). `./verify.sh` compiles. The serial command `model dino:Tyrannosaurus` opens the view without voice or AI, for the later trial.
- **The data is on the server.** Checked on 2026-11-14 over HTTPS against this tree: all 263 index files, `credits.html`, 60 random sheets and 30 random pictures are byte for byte the same, and the live `index.html` has the link to `credits.html` (item 15). Not checked: the other sheets and pictures one by one. Still open: timing the three requests against the 10 s on the stick.
- **When the data is generated again**, upload it as in Task C of the plan: sheets and pictures first without `--delete`, then the indexes with `--delete-after` on `data/dino/` only (so no old index names a deleted sheet; the generator empties the folder, so a dropped sheet must go from the server too). Upload the data before any firmware that needs it leaves the bench. A stick whose server has no sheet falls back to the picture and the "Einen Steckbrief dazu habe ich nicht." line.
